Aviation Accident Attorneys

Aviation Accidents Demand a Specialist — Not a General Practitioner

Aviation accident litigation is among the most technically complex and legally demanding fields in personal injury law. Unlike automobile accident cases, aviation claims involve federal jurisdiction through the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA), intricate product liability issues against aircraft and component manufacturers, international treaties when foreign carriers are involved, and liability chains that may span pilots, airlines, charter operators, maintenance companies, air traffic control, and government entities. The investigations are technically demanding, the defendants are well-resourced, and the evidentiary standards are rigorous. PM Law Firm has the experience to navigate this complexity and the determination to hold responsible parties accountable for the catastrophic injuries and deaths that aviation accidents cause.

Federal Jurisdiction and FAA Regulations

Aviation in the United States is regulated at the federal level, primarily by the Federal Aviation Administration under the authority of the Federal Aviation Act. This federal regulatory framework creates a complex jurisdictional landscape for aviation accident plaintiffs:

FAA Certification Standards

Every commercial aircraft, pilot, mechanic, and air traffic controller must meet specific FAA certification standards. Violations of these standards — including operating without proper certification, flying beyond approved limitations, or using non-certified parts — are powerful evidence of negligence.

Federal Preemption Issues

Federal aviation law preempts some state tort claims while leaving others available. Successfully navigating which claims are preempted and which are available under state or federal law requires specialized aviation litigation expertise.

NTSB Investigation

The National Transportation Safety Board investigates aviation accidents and issues factual and probable cause reports. While NTSB findings are not admissible as evidence of fault in civil litigation, the underlying factual findings and safety recommendations are enormously valuable investigative tools.

FAR Violations as Negligence Per Se

Violations of Federal Aviation Regulations (FARs) may constitute negligence per se under Texas law — meaning the violation itself establishes the duty and breach elements of negligence without requiring additional expert testimony on the standard of care.

Types of Aviation Accidents

PM Law Firm handles aviation accident claims arising from all types of aircraft and accident scenarios:

Commercial Airline Accidents

Accidents involving commercial passenger carriers are subject to intense regulatory scrutiny and may involve multiple defendants including the airline, aircraft manufacturer, and air traffic control authorities.

Private & General Aviation

Small private aircraft accidents frequently involve pilot error, mechanical failure, or airspace management issues. The FAA’s general aviation regulations impose specific maintenance and operation duties on aircraft owners.

Helicopter Accidents

Helicopters operate in unique and often hazardous environments — low altitudes, offshore platforms, emergency medical service flights, and air tours — each with specific regulatory requirements and liability considerations.

Charter & Air Taxi Accidents

Charter operators and air taxi services must comply with both commercial air carrier regulations (Part 135) and general aviation rules. Accidents often reveal systemic safety violations in these smaller operations.

Cargo Aircraft Accidents

Cargo carriers operate under Part 121 regulations and may carry dangerous goods requiring additional compliance measures. Accidents involving cargo aircraft can affect ground personnel as well as flight crews.

Airport Ground Accidents

Accidents involving ground vehicles, jet blast, propeller strikes, and boarding bridge failures on airport property may fall under both aviation and premises liability law, requiring knowledge of both frameworks.

Who Can Be Held Liable?

The aviation accident liability chain is often far more complex than in other types of accident cases. Potentially responsible parties include:

Pilot & Flight Crew

Pilots bear personal liability for negligent operation, failure to follow procedures, operating under the influence, fatigue violations, and exceeding aircraft limitations. Co-pilots and crew may also share liability for their own failures.

Airline or Charter Operator

The air carrier bears vicarious liability for pilot and crew negligence, as well as direct liability for negligent hiring, inadequate training, poor maintenance programs, and systemic safety culture failures.

Aircraft & Component Manufacturers

When a mechanical failure or design defect contributes to an aviation accident, the aircraft manufacturer, engine manufacturer, and component parts manufacturers may face strict product liability claims regardless of operator fault.

Maintenance & Repair Organizations

FAA-certified repair stations and individual mechanics who perform negligent maintenance, use unapproved parts, or fail to document their work properly may share liability for resulting accidents.

Air Traffic Control

The FAA’s air traffic control function is a government activity, meaning claims against ATC must be brought under the Federal Tort Claims Act with its special procedures and limitations.

Airport Operators

Airports that maintain runways, lighting, navigation aids, and ground support equipment must keep these systems in safe operating condition. Failures that contribute to accidents may give rise to premises liability claims.

Key Evidence in Aviation Cases

Aviation accident investigations generate unique categories of evidence that are critical to establishing liability. PM Law Firm moves quickly to secure and analyze:

Flight Data Recorder (Black Box)

Modern aircraft carry flight data recorders that capture hundreds of flight parameters in the moments before an accident. Analysis by qualified experts can pinpoint mechanical failures, deviations from procedure, and contributing factors.

Cockpit Voice Recorder

The CVR captures crew communications and cockpit sounds that reveal crew decision-making, adherence to procedures, distraction, and the sequence of events leading to the accident.

Maintenance Records

Aircraft maintenance logbooks, repair orders, airworthiness directives compliance records, and parts traceability documentation reveal whether the aircraft was airworthy at the time of the accident.

Air Traffic Control Recordings

ATC radar data, communication recordings, and operational logs document the aircraft’s position, altitude, speed, and all communications with controllers in the critical period before the accident.

Pilot Training & Qualification Records

FAA records, airline training files, simulator evaluations, and prior incident reports document the pilot’s proficiency, any known deficiencies, and the adequacy of the operator’s training program.

Weather Data

METAR reports, PIREPs, and weather radar data from the time of the accident document the meteorological conditions the crew knew or should have known about when making operational decisions.
