Documenting Your Car Accident

The evidence you gather immediately after a crash can make or break your case. Insurance adjusters arrive on scene quickly, and the defense will work hard to minimize what happened. Here is what you should do if you are physically able:

Photograph Everything

Take wide-angle and close-up photos of all vehicles, damage, skid marks, debris, traffic signals, road signs, and any visible injuries. Time-stamp your photos if possible.

Record Written Details

Write down the sequence of events while memory is fresh. Note the direction of travel, speed estimates, road conditions, and any traffic violations you observed the other driver commit.

Collect Witness Information

Independent witnesses are invaluable. Get the name, phone number, and email of every bystander who saw the crash before they leave the scene.

Document Weather & Lighting

Note weather conditions, time of day, visibility, and any environmental factors such as sun glare, standing water, or construction zones that may have contributed to the accident.

Never Give a Statement Without Your Lawyer Present

One of the most critical mistakes accident victims make is giving a recorded statement to the other driver’s insurance company without legal counsel. Adjusters are trained interrogators whose job is to get you to say something — anything — that can be used to reduce or eliminate your claim. Common traps include questions like “How are you feeling today?” (a “fine” answer can be used against you) or “Can you describe exactly what happened?” designed to pin you to an account before all facts are known. You have no legal obligation to speak with the other party’s insurer before consulting an attorney. Contact PM Law Firm first, and we will handle all communications on your behalf.

What Compensation Can You Recover?

Texas law allows car accident victims to pursue a broad range of damages. The full value of your case depends on the severity of your injuries, the impact on your life, and the degree of the other party’s fault. Recoverable damages typically include:

Medical Bills

All past and current medical expenses related to the accident — emergency room, surgery, hospitalization, specialist visits, physical therapy, chiropractic care, and prescription medications.

Lost Wages & Earning Capacity

Compensation for income lost while you recovered, plus future lost earning capacity if your injuries prevent you from returning to the same job or working at the same level.

Pain & Suffering

Non-economic damages for physical pain, emotional distress, anxiety, depression, loss of enjoyment of life, and the psychological impact of your injuries and recovery.

Property Damage

Full fair market value of your vehicle and all personal property damaged in the crash, including rental reimbursement while repairs are made or a replacement is arranged.

Future Medical Costs

If your injuries require ongoing care — additional surgeries, pain management, long-term therapy, or assistive devices — those future costs are part of your claim today.

Disfigurement & Impairment

Texas allows recovery for permanent scarring, disfigurement, and any lasting physical impairment that affects your day-to-day functioning and quality of life.

Common Causes of Car Accidents in Houston

Houston’s massive highway network, dense traffic, and unpredictable weather create conditions ripe for collisions. The most frequent causes we see in our cases include:

Distracted Driving

Texting, phone calls, navigation apps, eating, and other distractions take a driver’s eyes off the road and are now a leading cause of crashes nationwide.

Drunk & Impaired Driving

Alcohol and drug impairment dramatically slows reaction time and impairs judgment. DUI accidents often result in particularly severe injuries and significant damages.

Speeding

Excessive speed reduces stopping distance and dramatically increases crash severity. Houston’s highways see frequent high-speed collisions with catastrophic results.

Running Red Lights & Stop Signs

Intersection violations are among the deadliest types of accidents because they often result in T-bone collisions at full speed with no warning.

Hazardous Weather

Houston’s heavy rains and flooding cause slick roads, reduced visibility, and hydroplaning. Many drivers fail to adjust their speed and following distance appropriately.

Tailgating & Aggressive Driving

Following too closely leaves no room to stop safely. Aggressive driving behaviors like lane weaving and brake-checking create dangerous conditions for everyone on the road.
