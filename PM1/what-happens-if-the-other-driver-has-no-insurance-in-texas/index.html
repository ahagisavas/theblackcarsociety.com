What Happens If the Other Driver Has No Insurance in Texas?

Being hit by an uninsured driver doesn’t mean you’re out of options. Here’s what you need to know.

PM Law Firm

Texas law requires all drivers to carry minimum liability insurance, but a significant number of drivers on Texas roads are either uninsured or underinsured. Being hit by one of these drivers can feel like a dead end — but it doesn’t have to be.

Texas’s Uninsured Driver Problem

According to estimates, roughly 1 in 8 drivers in Texas is uninsured. That means every time you get on the road, there is a real possibility that another driver around you is operating without any coverage. Texas requires a minimum of $30,000 per person / $60,000 per accident in bodily injury liability coverage, but many drivers ignore this requirement entirely.

Uninsured Motorist (UM) Coverage

One of the most important protections available to Texas drivers is uninsured motorist (UM) coverage. While not required by law, insurers are required to offer it — and you must explicitly reject it in writing if you choose not to carry it. UM coverage pays for your medical bills, lost wages, and pain and suffering when the at-fault driver has no insurance. This coverage kicks in as your own insurance policy, up to your policy’s limits.

Underinsured Motorist (UIM) Coverage

If the at-fault driver has some insurance but not enough to cover your full damages, underinsured motorist (UIM) coverage can make up the difference. For example, if your damages total $100,000 but the at-fault driver only carries $30,000 in coverage, your UIM policy can cover the remaining $70,000 — up to your policy limits.

Suing an Uninsured Driver Directly

You can also pursue a personal injury lawsuit directly against the uninsured driver. The challenge here is collectability — even if you win a judgment, collecting from someone who cannot afford insurance can be difficult. However, in some cases it is worthwhile, particularly if the at-fault driver has assets or future income that can satisfy a judgment.

What to Do After an Accident with an Uninsured Driver

  • Call the police and get an accident report
  • Document the scene thoroughly with photos
  • Gather witness information
  • Notify your own insurance company promptly
  • Contact an experienced personal injury attorney before accepting any settlement offers
