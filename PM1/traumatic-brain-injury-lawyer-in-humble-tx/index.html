The Most Serious Injury You May Not Immediately Recognize

Traumatic brain injuries are among the most devastating consequences of accidents — and among the most frequently underestimated. Unlike a broken leg, a TBI may not be visible from the outside. Symptoms can develop gradually over hours or days, and victims sometimes walk away from accident scenes unaware that they have suffered a serious brain injury. By the time the full impact becomes clear — cognitive difficulties, personality changes, chronic headaches, memory loss — critical medical and legal windows may have closed. Types of Traumatic Brain Injuries

TBIs exist on a wide spectrum of severity. Even injuries classified as “mild” can produce symptoms that last months or years and significantly impair a person’s ability to work and function in daily life.

Concussion

The most common form of TBI, caused by a blow to the head or violent shaking of the skull. Concussions disrupt normal brain function and can cause headaches, confusion, dizziness, memory problems, and sensitivity to light and sound. Repeated concussions carry compounding risk.

Cerebral Contusion

A bruise on the brain tissue itself, caused by direct impact. Contusions are more severe than concussions and can cause bleeding, swelling, and lasting neurological deficits depending on the location and size of the affected area.

Diffuse Axonal Injury

One of the most severe and difficult-to-diagnose TBIs, caused by the brain twisting or rotating violently within the skull during rapid acceleration or deceleration. DAI can cause widespread nerve fiber damage that does not always appear on standard CT scans.

Penetrating TBI

Occurs when an object breaks through the skull and enters the brain tissue. These injuries are among the most catastrophic and life-threatening and often result in permanent neurological damage, paralysis, or death.

Symptoms and Long-Term Effects

The consequences of a TBI touch every dimension of a victim’s life. Symptoms vary based on the severity and location of the brain injury and can be temporary or permanent:

Cognitive Effects

Difficulty concentrating, memory impairment, slowed processing speed, confusion, difficulty with problem-solving, and diminished executive function that affects job performance and daily decision-making.

Physical Effects

Persistent headaches, migraines, dizziness, nausea, fatigue, sleep disorders, seizures, vision and hearing problems, and loss of coordination or motor control.

Emotional & Behavioral Effects

Irritability, mood swings, depression, anxiety, aggression, impulsivity, social withdrawal, and personality changes that can strain relationships and isolate victims from their support networks.

Sensory Effects

Tinnitus (ringing in the ears), hypersensitivity to light and sound, loss of smell or taste, changes in speech and language, and difficulty with reading and writing.

Calculating the True Cost of a TBI

TBI claims are uniquely complex to value because the true economic impact of the injury extends far into the future. A competent TBI attorney must account for every dimension of this cost:

Immediate Medical Expenses

Emergency care, CT scans, MRIs, hospitalization, neurosurgery, intensive care, and initial rehabilitation all generate substantial bills that must be fully documented and included in your claim.

Future Medical Costs

TBI rehabilitation is often a years-long process involving neuropsychology, occupational therapy, speech therapy, and medication management. We work with life care planners to project these costs accurately over your lifetime.

Lost Earning Capacity

If your TBI prevents you from returning to your former occupation or reduces your ability to work at the same level, you are entitled to compensation for the difference in earning capacity over your remaining working life.

Non-Economic Damages

Pain and suffering, loss of enjoyment of life, loss of cognitive identity, and the strain on personal relationships are real and compensable damages that our attorneys fight hard to have properly valued.

Why Insurance Companies Undervalue TBI Claims

Insurance companies deploy specific tactics to minimize TBI claim payouts. They know that TBIs are difficult to visualize and that some victims — especially those with mild to moderate injuries — may appear functional from the outside. Their typical strategy includes challenging the causation of the injury (arguing the symptoms predate the accident), disputing the severity (“mild TBI does not cause these symptoms”), attacking treating physicians as biased, and offering quick settlements before the full extent of the injury is known. PM Law Firm counters each of these tactics with documented medical evidence, independent expert opinions, and rigorous legal preparation.
