Who Can File a Wrongful Death Claim in Texas?

Under the Texas Wrongful Death Act, a wrongful death action may be filed by specific surviving family members. Texas law identifies the following eligible claimants:

Surviving Spouse

The surviving husband or wife of the deceased has standing to bring a wrongful death claim regardless of whether a will existed and may recover for both economic losses and the loss of companionship and consortium.

Children

Both biological and legally adopted children of the deceased have standing. Adult children, as well as minor children, may bring claims. The loss of parental guidance, love, and financial support are all compensable elements.

Parents

The biological or adoptive parents of the deceased may file a wrongful death claim, even if the deceased was an adult. Parents may recover for the loss of companionship, mental anguish, and, in some cases, financial support.

Personal Representative

If eligible family members do not file a wrongful death claim within three months of the death, the personal representative (executor) of the deceased’s estate may file on behalf of surviving relatives.

Types of Wrongful Death Cases

Wrongful death claims arise in many contexts. PM Law Firm handles all types of fatal accident cases, including:

Fatal Car Accidents

Drunk drivers, distracted drivers, and reckless speeders take lives on Houston roads every day. We pursue full wrongful death and survival claims against responsible drivers and their insurers.

Fatal Truck Accidents

Commercial truck crashes are disproportionately deadly. We investigate carriers, drivers, and associated parties to identify all sources of liability and maximum insurance coverage.

Workplace Fatalities

When an employee is killed due to unsafe working conditions or a third party’s negligence — not just employer negligence covered by workers’ comp — a civil wrongful death claim may be available.

Medical Negligence

When a doctor, hospital, or healthcare provider’s negligence directly causes a patient’s death, surviving family members may bring a medical malpractice-based wrongful death action under Texas law.

Premises Liability Deaths

Negligent property maintenance, inadequate security, or unsafe conditions that cause fatal falls, drownings, or other accidents on another party’s property may support a wrongful death claim.

Defective Product Deaths

When a defectively designed or manufactured product — including vehicles, safety equipment, or consumer goods — causes a fatal accident, the manufacturer may be held strictly liable for wrongful death.

Damages Recoverable in a Wrongful Death Claim

Texas law allows the surviving family members to recover a broad range of damages in a wrongful death action. These include both economic and non-economic losses:

Funeral & Burial Expenses

The reasonable cost of funeral, burial or cremation, and memorial services may be recovered from the responsible party as part of the wrongful death claim.

Lost Financial Support

The present value of the income and financial contributions the deceased would have made to the family over their expected working life, including employee benefits, retirement contributions, and other financial support.

Loss of Companionship & Society

Spouses and children may recover for the profound loss of love, affection, comfort, companionship, and the nurturing relationship they shared with the deceased — damages that are real even though they are not economic.

Mental Anguish

The grief, emotional suffering, and psychological trauma of losing a loved one to negligence is compensable under Texas wrongful death law for eligible surviving family members.

Loss of Parental Guidance

Minor children who lose a parent are entitled to recover for the loss of the guidance, counsel, education, and parental care they would have received throughout their childhood and young adulthood.

Survival Claim Damages

A separate survival action may be brought on behalf of the deceased’s estate for the pain, suffering, and medical expenses the deceased experienced between the incident and their death.

Texas Statute of Limitations

Under Texas law, wrongful death claims must generally be filed within two years of the date of the deceased’s death. This two-year statute of limitations is strictly enforced. Missing the deadline almost always bars the family from recovering any compensation, regardless of how clear the liability is. There are limited exceptions — for example, when the responsible party fraudulently concealed their conduct — but these are narrow and difficult to establish.
