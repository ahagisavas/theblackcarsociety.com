How to Know If Insurance Is Underpaying You for an Auto Accident

Insurance companies routinely offer less than claims are worth. Here’s how to spot it — and what to do.

PM Law Firm

After a car accident, most people expect their insurance claim to be handled fairly. Unfortunately, insurance companies are for-profit businesses with a financial incentive to pay as little as possible on every claim. Knowing the signs that you are being underpaid can make the difference between a fair settlement and one that leaves you struggling to cover your losses.

Signs Your Settlement Offer Is Too Low

  • The offer does not cover all your medical bills — past or future
  • The adjuster contacted you very quickly after the accident with an offer
  • The offer does not include compensation for pain and suffering
  • You are still receiving medical treatment but the insurer wants to settle now
  • The adjuster downplays your injuries or questions whether they were caused by the accident
  • The offer does not account for lost wages or reduced earning capacity

How Insurance Companies Calculate — and Minimize — Settlements

Insurers use proprietary software and formulas to calculate settlement offers. These systems are designed to generate low initial offers. Adjusters are trained to resolve claims quickly and cheaply. They may downplay the severity of your injuries, argue that some treatment was unnecessary, or claim that pre-existing conditions are responsible for your current symptoms.

What Your Claim May Actually Be Worth

A fair personal injury settlement should account for:

  • All medical expenses (past and future)
  • Lost wages and lost earning capacity
  • Vehicle repair or replacement
  • Pain and suffering
  • Emotional distress
  • Loss of enjoyment of life

Insurance companies rarely volunteer these full categories of damages in their initial offers.

What to Do If You Think You’re Being Underpaid

Do not sign any release or accept any offer without consulting an attorney. Once you sign, you typically cannot seek additional compensation. An experienced personal injury attorney can review your settlement offer, calculate the true value of your claim, and negotiate aggressively on your behalf — or file suit if necessary.
