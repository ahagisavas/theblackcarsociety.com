Your Insurer Has a Legal Duty to Deal Fairly With You

Every insurance policy is a contract — and every insurance company in Texas has a legal duty to handle claims in good faith. When an insurer deliberately denies a valid claim, unreasonably delays payment, misrepresents policy terms, or uses other unfair tactics to avoid paying what it owes, it may be committing insurance bad faith. In Texas, insurance bad faith is not just a breach of contract — it is a violation of the Texas Insurance Code and the Texas Deceptive Trade Practices Act that can expose the insurer to additional damages beyond your original claim amount. PM Law Firm holds insurers accountable throughout Humble, Houston, and Harris County. We know the law, we know the tactics, and we fight back effectively.

What Is Insurance Bad Faith in Texas?

Insurance bad faith occurs when an insurer fails to fulfill its legal and contractual obligations to a policyholder without a reasonable basis for doing so. Texas law imposes both common-law and statutory duties on insurance companies that go beyond simply honoring the terms of the policy:

Duty to Promptly Investigate

Insurers must conduct a reasonable, thorough, and timely investigation of every claim. Using delay tactics to avoid investigation or rushing to denial without adequate inquiry is a form of bad faith.

Duty to Pay Valid Claims

When an investigation reveals that a claim is covered under the policy, the insurer must pay it promptly and fully. Offering token payments on clearly covered claims to pressure claimants into accepting less constitutes bad faith.

Duty to Communicate Honestly

Insurers may not misrepresent policy terms, coverage provisions, or claim status to policyholders. Telling a claimant their claim is not covered when it plainly is violates both the Texas Insurance Code and DTPA.

Duty of Good Faith & Fair Dealing

Texas common law imposes a duty of good faith and fair dealing on all first-party insurance relationships. An insurer that denies a claim when it knows or should know there is no reasonable basis for denial violates this duty.

Texas Insurance Code Requirements

The Texas Insurance Code establishes specific mandatory timelines and requirements for claim handling. Violations of these statutory provisions give policyholders the right to additional statutory damages and attorney’s fees:

Prompt Acknowledgment

Insurers must acknowledge receipt of a claim within 15 days and begin investigation promptly. Failure to acknowledge or respond within this window is a statutory violation.

Timely Acceptance or Denial

After receiving all items, statements, and forms reasonably required, insurers must accept or deny the claim within 15 business days. Extensions are limited and must be communicated in writing.

Prompt Payment

Once a claim is accepted, insurers must pay within 5 business days. Delays in payment after acceptance trigger penalty interest at 18% per year plus attorney’s fees under the Texas Prompt Payment of Claims Act.

Written Denial Reasons

Any denial of a claim must be in writing and must specifically state the factual and policy basis for the denial. Vague or pretextual denial letters violate the Texas Insurance Code.

Common Bad Faith Tactics Insurers Use

After handling hundreds of insurance disputes in Texas, we have seen insurers use the same playbook repeatedly. Recognizing these tactics is the first step in fighting back:

Claim Stonewalling

Repeatedly requesting the same information already provided, losing submitted documentation, or simply failing to respond to calls and emails in order to wear down claimants until they give up or accept a pittance.

Biased Inspections

Sending company-favored inspectors or adjusters who systematically undervalue damage, exclude pre-existing conditions selectively, or apply policy exclusions in ways that are inconsistent with the actual policy language.

Coverage Misrepresentation

Telling policyholders their claim is not covered, that certain events are excluded, or that their policy has limitations that do not actually exist — counting on policyholders not to scrutinize the actual policy language.

Lowball Settlement Pressure

Making initial settlement offers that represent a fraction of the claim’s actual value, then pressuring injured or distressed claimants to accept quickly before they fully understand the extent of their losses.

Additional Damages Available in Bad Faith Cases

One of the most powerful aspects of Texas insurance bad faith law is that a successful claimant may recover far more than just the original policy benefit. Additional available damages include:

18% Penalty Interest

Under the Texas Prompt Payment of Claims Act, insurers who delay payment after acceptance owe 18% annual interest on the unpaid amount — a significant additional recovery over time.

Attorney’s Fees

Both the Texas Insurance Code and DTPA provide for recovery of attorney’s fees when the insurer violates its statutory obligations, making it economically feasible to fight back regardless of claim size.

Treble Damages Under DTPA

If the insurer’s bad faith conduct constitutes a knowing or intentional violation of the Deceptive Trade Practices Act, the court may award up to three times the actual damages suffered.

Extracontractual Damages

Beyond the policy benefit itself, policyholders may recover consequential damages caused by the insurer’s wrongful delay or denial — such as additional property damage caused by a delayed repair payment.
