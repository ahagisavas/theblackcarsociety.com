FedEx Is a Legal Powerhouse — You Need Powerful Representation

FedEx Corporation operates one of the largest vehicle fleets in the world, with hundreds of thousands of delivery vehicles, tractor-trailers, and freight trucks logging millions of miles across Texas every year. In the greater Houston area alone, FedEx Ground, FedEx Freight, FedEx Express, and FedEx Office vehicles move through residential neighborhoods, industrial corridors, and major highways every single day. When one of these vehicles strikes your car, the consequences can be life-altering — and FedEx’s legal and insurance teams respond immediately to limit the company’s exposure. PM Law Firm has specific experience handling FedEx accident claims, and we respond with equal urgency to protect your rights.

FedEx Contractor vs. Employee — Why It Matters

One of FedEx Ground’s most legally significant characteristics is its use of independent service providers (ISPs) rather than direct employees to make package deliveries. FedEx Ground deliberately structures its Ground network to use contracted drivers operating under the umbrella of ISP companies. FedEx has historically argued that because these drivers are technically employed by ISP contractors rather than FedEx directly, FedEx bears no liability when one of them causes an accident.

This argument fails in many circumstances. Courts across the country — and Texas law — recognize that when a company exercises sufficient operational control over a contractor’s work, a genuine employment or agency relationship exists regardless of the label. FedEx dictates routes, delivery schedules, vehicle markings, uniforms, scanning procedures, and performance metrics for its Ground drivers. This level of control frequently establishes FedEx’s liability even for accidents caused by nominally “independent” contractors. Our attorneys know exactly how to argue this issue and have the case law to support it.

How FedEx Limits Its Liability

FedEx employs a sophisticated legal strategy designed to minimize payouts after accidents. Understanding these tactics is the first step in defeating them:

The Contractor Shield

FedEx Ground immediately points to its ISP contractor model to deflect liability. Their lawyers argue that the ISP — not FedEx — is the responsible party, often leaving victims chasing a smaller entity with limited insurance coverage.

Rapid Evidence Preservation

FedEx dispatches representatives and adjusters to accident scenes quickly. Their goal is to document the scene from their perspective, download vehicle data, and secure delivery logs before you have an attorney. You must act fast.

Quick Low-Ball Settlements

Adjusters often contact victims within days offering fast settlements. These initial offers are invariably far below the true value of the claim. Once you sign a release, you waive all future rights to additional compensation.

Aggressive Defense Litigation

If a case proceeds to litigation, FedEx retains specialized trucking defense firms with enormous resources. They will depose every witness, challenge every medical bill, and file every available motion to delay or reduce your recovery.

Evidence That Wins FedEx Cases

The foundation of a successful FedEx accident claim is comprehensive, rapidly secured evidence. Our attorneys move quickly to preserve:

Dashcam & Traffic Camera Footage

FedEx vehicles are increasingly equipped with dashcams, and many Houston intersections have traffic cameras. This footage is often overwritten within days — a litigation hold must be issued immediately.

Delivery Logs & Dispatch Records

FedEx’s proprietary tracking system logs every scan, pickup, and delivery. This data reveals whether a driver was under time pressure, was in the wrong location, or deviated from an assigned route.

Driver Qualification Records

We subpoena the driver’s CDL history, drug testing records, prior accident reports, and training documentation from both the ISP and FedEx to identify prior safety violations.

GPS & Telematics Data

Modern FedEx vehicles generate detailed telematics data including speed, braking events, acceleration patterns, and location history — all of which can reconstruct exactly how the accident happened.

Vehicle Maintenance Records

We request all inspection and maintenance logs for the specific vehicle involved to identify whether deferred repairs or known defects contributed to the crash.

ISP & FedEx Contracts

The service agreements between FedEx and its ISPs reveal the true extent of FedEx’s operational control — the key to establishing corporate liability beyond the contractor entity.

Types of FedEx Vehicles

FedEx operates multiple distinct divisions, each with different vehicle types, operating structures, and insurance programs. Understanding which division was involved is critical to identifying the correct defendants and insurance policies.

FedEx Ground

The package delivery arm of FedEx, operating primarily through ISP contractors in cargo vans, sprinter vans, and box trucks. Most residential and business package delivery accidents involve this division.

FedEx Freight

Operates large tractor-trailers for less-than-truckload (LTL) freight shipments. These vehicles are the heaviest in the FedEx fleet and cause the most catastrophic injuries when they are involved in accidents.

FedEx Express

Operates a mix of vans, box trucks, and aircraft for time-sensitive overnight and priority deliveries. Express drivers are typically direct FedEx employees rather than ISP contractors.

FedEx Office

Operates vans for document and printing services pickup and delivery. While smaller vehicles, these can still cause serious injuries, particularly to pedestrians, cyclists, and motorcyclists.

What to Do After a FedEx Truck Accident

Your actions in the hours and days immediately following a FedEx accident can significantly affect the value of your claim. Follow these steps:

Call 911

Always call police and get an official accident report. Note the responding officer’s name and badge number and request a copy of the report as soon as it is available.

Document the Scene

Photograph the FedEx vehicle, including its markings, license plate, and any FedEx Ground contractor markings. These identify the specific ISP and policy involved.

Seek Medical Care Immediately

Even if you feel fine, get a medical evaluation the same day. Many serious injuries — including TBI and internal bleeding — do not produce obvious symptoms immediately after the crash.

Do Not Speak to FedEx Adjusters

Politely decline to give any statement to FedEx or its insurance representatives until you have spoken with an attorney. Refer all communications to PM Law Firm.

Call PM Law Firm

Contact us as soon as possible so we can issue a litigation hold to FedEx, preserve electronic evidence, and begin building your case before critical evidence disappears.

Keep All Records

Save every medical bill, treatment record, prescription receipt, and note from your doctors. Keep a journal documenting your pain levels, limitations, and how your injuries affect your daily life.
