When Someone Else’s Negligence Changes Your Life, We Fight Back

Personal injury law exists for a fundamental reason: when one person’s careless or reckless conduct causes another person harm, the law holds that person — and often their employer or insurer — financially responsible for the consequences. We handle the full spectrum of personal injury cases, from car and truck accidents to slip and falls, workplace injuries, and more. Our attorneys combine decades of Texas courtroom experience with a client-first approach that keeps you informed and empowered at every step of the process.

What Is Negligence?

To succeed in a Texas personal injury claim, you must establish that the defendant was negligent. Negligence has four elements, all of which must be proven:

Duty of Care

The defendant owed you a legal duty of care. Drivers owe a duty of reasonable care to other road users. Property owners owe duties to visitors. Manufacturers owe duties to end consumers. The specific duty depends on the relationship between the parties.

Breach of Duty

The defendant breached that duty by acting (or failing to act) in a way that a reasonably prudent person in the same situation would not have. Running a red light, ignoring a known hazard, and manufacturing a defective product are all examples of breach.

Causation

The defendant’s breach of duty must have actually and proximately caused your injury. There must be a direct causal chain between their negligent conduct and the harm you suffered, not merely a temporal coincidence.

Damages

You must have suffered actual, measurable harm as a result — physical injury, financial loss, or both. Without demonstrable damages, there is no viable personal injury claim even if negligence occurred.

What Damages Can You Recover?

Texas personal injury law distinguishes between economic and non-economic damages. Both categories are recoverable in most cases:

Economic Damages

Quantifiable financial losses including all past and future medical expenses, lost wages, reduced earning capacity, property damage, and any out-of-pocket expenses caused by the injury.

Non-Economic Damages

Harder to quantify but equally real: physical pain and suffering, emotional distress, mental anguish, loss of enjoyment of life, loss of consortium, and permanent disfigurement or impairment.

Exemplary Damages

In cases involving gross negligence or intentional misconduct, Texas law permits exemplary (punitive) damages designed to punish the defendant and deter similar conduct in the future.

Future Damages

If your injuries require ongoing or future medical care, vocational rehabilitation, or have permanently reduced your ability to earn income, those future losses are recoverable today as part of your current claim.

Texas Statute of Limitations

In Texas, personal injury claims must generally be filed within two years of the date the injury occurred. This is a hard deadline: missing the statute of limitations almost always results in your claim being permanently barred, regardless of how clear the defendant’s fault may be. Limited exceptions exist — for example, when the injured party is a minor, when the defendant fraudulently concealed information, or when the injury was not immediately discoverable — but these exceptions are narrow and must be specifically argued. The moment you suspect you have a personal injury claim, contact PM Law Firm. Early action protects your legal rights and preserves the evidence necessary to win your case.
