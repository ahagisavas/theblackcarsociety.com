Cyclists Have Rights — And We Fight to Protect Them

Bicyclists share Texas roads with vehicles that outweigh them by thousands of pounds, operated by drivers who frequently fail to see, anticipate, or respect the presence of cyclists. When a car or truck strikes a bicycle, the cyclist almost always suffers the most severe consequences — traumatic brain injuries, severe orthopedic fractures, road rash, and worse — while the driver may escape with little more than a dented bumper. Insurance companies then frequently attempt to blame the cyclist, challenge the severity of the injuries, or point to perceived traffic violations to minimize or deny the claim. Texas Bicycle Laws

Under Texas law, bicycles are vehicles, and cyclists have the same rights and responsibilities on public roadways as motor vehicle operators. Key provisions of Texas bicycle law include:

Right to Use the Roadway

Texas law (Transportation Code §551.101) gives cyclists the right to operate on all public roadways, including most highways, subject to specific exceptions. Drivers who claim cyclists had no right to be on the road are generally wrong as a matter of law.

Three-Foot Passing Law

Texas law requires drivers to give cyclists at least three feet of clearance when passing. Drivers who pass too closely are in violation of state law, and that violation is powerful evidence of negligence in a bicycle accident case.

Right-of-Way Rules

Cyclists have the same right-of-way rights as any other vehicle. Drivers who fail to yield at intersections, make left turns across a cyclist’s path, or cut off a cyclist have violated the cyclist’s right of way and bear liability for resulting injuries.

Bicycle Lanes & Paths

When a designated bicycle lane exists, cyclists may be required to use it. However, drivers must not enter bicycle lanes except when turning, and failure to respect bicycle lane boundaries is a traffic violation that supports a negligence claim.

Common Causes of Bicycle Accidents

The overwhelming majority of serious bicycle accidents are caused by driver negligence, not cyclist error. The most frequent causes we see in our cases include:

Dooring

A driver or passenger opens a car door directly into a cyclist’s path without checking. The impact can throw the cyclist off their bike at speed, causing severe injuries. Both the opener and the driver may be liable.

Right-Hook Turns

A driver turns right at an intersection, cutting across a cyclist who is traveling straight. This is one of the most common and dangerous intersection conflicts for cyclists in urban environments.

Left-Cross Collisions

A driver turns left across an intersection directly into the path of an oncoming cyclist. The driver fails to see or misjudges the speed of the approaching cyclist, resulting in a high-speed broadside collision.

Distracted Drivers

Drivers using phones, navigation systems, or other distractions are particularly dangerous to cyclists because cyclists present a smaller visual profile and require more active attention to notice.

Road Hazards

Potholes, storm drain grates parallel to traffic, sand and gravel deposits, cracked pavement, and poorly maintained bike lanes can cause cyclists to lose control. Municipal entities may be liable for negligent road maintenance.

Rear-End Collisions

Distracted or speeding drivers who fail to notice a cyclist ahead and rear-end them typically cause the most catastrophic bicycle accident injuries, particularly head and spinal cord trauma.

Types of Injuries in Bicycle Accidents

Without the structural protection of a vehicle, cyclists who are struck by cars or trucks are exposed to the full force of the collision. Typical injuries in bicycle accidents include:

Fractures

Broken wrists, arms, collarbones, ankles, and legs are among the most common bicycle accident injuries. Many require surgical repair with hardware fixation, followed by months of physical therapy and rehabilitation.

Traumatic Brain Injury

Even helmeted cyclists can suffer TBIs in high-energy collisions. TBIs range from concussions with weeks-long recovery to severe brain damage causing permanent cognitive and functional impairment.

Road Rash

Sliding across pavement causes deep abrasions that can penetrate through multiple skin layers, require wound debridement, and leave permanent scarring particularly when covering large areas of the body.

Spinal Cord Injuries

The impact of being struck by a vehicle and then hitting the ground can cause herniated discs, vertebral fractures, and in the most serious cases, partial or complete spinal cord injuries with lasting neurological consequences.

Helmet Use and Comparative Fault in Texas

Texas has no statewide law requiring adults to wear bicycle helmets, although some municipalities have local ordinances. If you were not wearing a helmet at the time of a bicycle accident, the defense will almost certainly argue that your failure to wear a helmet constitutes contributory negligence that should reduce your recovery — particularly for head injuries. PM Law Firm addresses this issue directly by working with medical experts who can establish the specific mechanism of your head injury and demonstrate that a helmet would not have prevented it, or alternatively, that Texas’s comparative fault framework appropriately limits any helmet-related reduction while ensuring that the majority of liability remains with the negligent driver who caused the crash.

Uninsured Motorist Coverage for Cyclists

A significant percentage of drivers who cause bicycle accidents are uninsured or underinsured. If you were struck by an uninsured driver, your own automobile insurance policy’s uninsured motorist (UM) coverage may cover your bicycle accident injuries even though you were not in your car at the time of the accident. Texas courts have generally held that UM coverage applies when a pedestrian or cyclist covered under the policy is struck by an uninsured motorist. PM Law Firm reviews all available insurance coverage — the at-fault driver’s policy, your own auto policy, and any applicable umbrella coverage — to identify every available source of compensation for your injuries.

What to Do After a Bicycle Accident

The actions you take in the immediate aftermath of a bicycle accident significantly affect the strength of your legal claim:

Call 911

Always call police to the scene of a bicycle accident involving a motor vehicle. An official police report creates a contemporaneous record of the collision that is important evidence in your claim.

Photograph the Scene

Photograph your bicycle, the vehicle, its license plate, all damage, road conditions, skid marks, bicycle lane markings, and your injuries before anything is moved or cleaned up.

Preserve Your Equipment

Do not repair or discard your damaged bicycle, helmet, or clothing. These items are physical evidence that can help establish the speed and angle of impact during accident reconstruction.

Seek Immediate Medical Care

Get evaluated at an emergency room or urgent care the same day, even if your injuries seem minor. Hidden injuries including TBI and internal bleeding may not be apparent immediately after a bicycle collision.
