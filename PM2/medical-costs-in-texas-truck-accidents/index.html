Medical Costs in Texas Truck Accidents

Truck accident injuries are often catastrophic — and the medical bills to match. Here’s what you may be entitled to recover.

PM Law Firm

Truck accidents are among the most devastating types of vehicle collisions. The sheer size and weight of commercial trucks — often 20 to 30 times heavier than a passenger vehicle — means that when they crash, the injuries are frequently catastrophic. Medical costs in serious truck accident cases can easily reach hundreds of thousands or even millions of dollars over a victim’s lifetime.

Types of Injuries Common in Truck Accidents

  • Traumatic brain injuries (TBI)
  • Spinal cord injuries and paralysis
  • Broken bones and crush injuries
  • Internal organ damage
  • Severe burns
  • Amputations
  • Wrongful death

These injuries often require immediate emergency care, multiple surgeries, extended hospital stays, intensive rehabilitation, and long-term or permanent medical support.

Immediate Medical Costs

Immediately following a truck accident, victims may face:

  • Ambulance and emergency room fees
  • Diagnostic imaging (MRI, CT scans, X-rays)
  • Emergency surgery
  • ICU stays
  • Specialist consultations

Emergency medical care alone can generate tens of thousands of dollars in bills within the first 24 to 48 hours.

Ongoing and Long-Term Medical Costs

Many truck accident injuries require treatment that extends months or years beyond the initial incident. This can include:

  • Physical therapy and rehabilitation
  • Follow-up surgeries
  • Prescription medications
  • Assistive devices (wheelchairs, prosthetics)
  • In-home nursing care
  • Psychological counseling
  • Vocational rehabilitation

These ongoing costs are a critical component of your total claim and must be carefully documented and projected.

How Future Medical Costs Are Calculated

Calculating future medical expenses in a truck accident case requires expert testimony from medical professionals and economists. Experts consider your current condition, your long-term prognosis, the cost of future procedures and care, inflation, and your life expectancy. This analysis forms the basis for the future damages portion of your claim.

Who Pays the Medical Bills?

In a truck accident case, multiple parties may be liable for your medical costs:

  • The truck driver
  • The trucking company
  • The cargo loading company
  • The vehicle manufacturer (if a defect contributed)
  • Potentially others in the chain of liability
