Riders Deserve the Same Rights as Every Other Road User

Motorcyclists face a dangerous combination of physical vulnerability and institutional bias when they are injured in accidents. A collision that might cause minor property damage between two cars can be fatal or catastrophic for a rider who has no metal cage protecting them. Then, when it comes time to pursue compensation, insurers and defense attorneys exploit cultural stereotypes about motorcyclists — suggesting riders are reckless thrill-seekers who assumed the risk of their injuries. PM Law Firm rejects that bias completely. We represent injured motorcyclists throughout Humble, Houston, and the greater Harris County area with the same aggressive advocacy we bring to every case. You were on the road legally, and you have the same right to compensation as any other accident victim.

Common Causes of Motorcycle Accidents in Texas

The vast majority of motorcycle accidents are caused by the negligence of other drivers, not by the motorcyclist. The most frequent causes we see in our cases include:

Failure to Yield

Car and truck drivers frequently fail to check properly for motorcycles before turning left, changing lanes, or pulling into traffic. “I didn’t see him” is not a legal defense — it is an admission of inattentive driving.

Distracted Driving

Drivers using phones, navigation devices, or other distractions are less likely to notice a motorcycle in their path because motorcycles present a smaller visual profile than other vehicles.

Unsafe Lane Changes

Motorcycles are particularly vulnerable when cars change lanes without checking blind spots. A side-swipe that barely registers for a car driver can throw a motorcyclist off their bike at highway speed.

Road Hazards

Potholes, gravel, debris, standing water, and uneven pavement that merely inconvenience car drivers can cause motorcyclists to lose control completely. Government entities or contractors may be liable for hazardous road conditions.

Texas Helmet Laws and How They Affect Your Case

Texas requires motorcycle operators and passengers to wear helmets unless the rider is 21 years of age or older AND either holds a motorcycle operator license and has completed a safety course, or is covered by a medical insurance plan providing at least $10,000 in medical coverage. If you were not wearing a helmet and were not legally exempt, the defense may argue that your head injuries would have been less severe with a helmet — and attempt to reduce your damages under Texas’s comparative fault rules. PM Law Firm is experienced in countering these arguments, working with medical experts to demonstrate the specific cause and severity of your injuries regardless of helmet use, and ensuring that the negligent driver’s fault is not obscured by irrelevant attacks on your safety choices.

Insurance Company Bias Against Motorcyclists

Insurance adjusters and defense attorneys frequently attempt to exploit negative stereotypes about motorcycle riders to reduce claim values. Common tactics include:

Speed Assumption

Without evidence, adjusters assume riders were traveling at excessive speed. We counter this with accident reconstruction experts, GPS data, and independent witness testimony.

Assumed Recklessness

Any prior traffic citation — even unrelated to the accident — may be used to paint the rider as generally reckless. We work to keep irrelevant prior history out of settlement negotiations and out of the courtroom.

Comparative Fault Manipulation

Texas uses modified comparative fault, meaning your recovery is reduced by your percentage of fault. Insurers inflate the rider’s assigned fault percentage to reduce their payout. We fight every unjustified fault allocation.

Minimizing Injury Severity

Because road rash and orthopedic injuries are common motorcycle accident outcomes, some adjusters treat them as minor. We ensure our clients’ injuries are fully documented and properly valued by medical experts.

Types of Injuries in Motorcycle Accidents

Because motorcyclists lack the structural protection of an enclosed vehicle, motorcycle accident injuries tend to be severe and often require extensive, costly treatment:

Road Rash

When a rider slides across pavement, the friction causes deep abrasions that can penetrate through multiple layers of skin and tissue, requiring debridement, skin grafts, and carrying significant risk of infection and permanent scarring.

Fractures & Orthopedic Injuries

Broken arms, legs, wrists, ankles, collarbones, and pelvis are common in motorcycle accidents. Many of these fractures are complex and require surgical repair, hardware implantation, and months of physical rehabilitation.

Traumatic Brain Injury

Even helmeted riders can suffer TBIs from the violent impact of a motorcycle accident. Unhelmeted riders face dramatically increased TBI risk. These injuries can result in permanent cognitive and functional impairment.

Spinal Cord Injuries

The forces involved in a motorcycle crash can cause herniated discs, vertebral fractures, and in the most severe cases, partial or complete spinal cord damage resulting in paralysis or permanent neurological deficits.

Comparative Fault and Motorcycle Accidents

Texas follows a modified comparative fault system. Under this rule, an injured party can recover damages as long as their share of fault does not exceed 50%. However, any percentage of fault assigned to the injured party directly reduces their recovery. For example, if you are found 20% at fault and your damages are $200,000, you recover $160,000. Insurance companies understand this system and will attempt to assign as much fault as possible to the motorcyclist to reduce their liability. Our attorneys aggressively challenge every unjustified fault allocation and present comprehensive evidence — accident reconstruction, eyewitness accounts, and electronic data — to ensure the at-fault driver bears the full measure of their responsibility.
