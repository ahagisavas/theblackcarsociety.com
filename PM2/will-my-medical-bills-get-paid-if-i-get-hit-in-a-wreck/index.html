Will My Medical Bills Get Paid If I Get Hit in a Wreck?

Medical bills pile up fast after an accident. Here’s how the payment process actually works in Texas.

PM Law Firm

One of the first questions people ask after being injured in a car accident is: who is going to pay my medical bills? The answer depends on several factors, including the type of insurance coverage available, the fault of each driver, and the severity of your injuries.

The At-Fault Driver’s Liability Insurance

In Texas, the at-fault driver’s liability insurance is generally responsible for paying your medical expenses. Texas follows a fault-based system, meaning the driver who caused the accident is financially responsible for the damages they caused. However, there is a significant catch: you typically do not receive payment from the at-fault driver’s insurance until your claim is settled or a judgment is entered — which can take months or even years.

Your Own Health Insurance

While you wait for the liability claim to resolve, your own health insurance can and should be used to pay for your medical treatment. This ensures you receive timely care without delay. When your personal injury claim settles, the health insurer may have a right to be reimbursed from your settlement (called a subrogation lien) — but an experienced attorney can often negotiate these liens down significantly.

Personal Injury Protection (PIP) Coverage

Texas drivers can purchase Personal Injury Protection (PIP) coverage, which pays for medical expenses and lost wages regardless of who was at fault. PIP kicks in quickly and can help cover immediate treatment costs while your liability claim is pending. PIP is not required in Texas, but insurers must offer it and you must reject it in writing if you don’t want it.

Medical Payment (MedPay) Coverage

MedPay is similar to PIP in that it covers medical expenses regardless of fault. It is typically less expensive than PIP and does not cover lost wages, but it can provide an important financial cushion in the immediate aftermath of an accident.

What If the At-Fault Driver Is Uninsured?

If the at-fault driver has no insurance, your options include:

  • Your own UM/UIM coverage (if you have it)
  • Your health insurance
  • PIP or MedPay coverage
  • Potentially a direct lawsuit against the at-fault driver
