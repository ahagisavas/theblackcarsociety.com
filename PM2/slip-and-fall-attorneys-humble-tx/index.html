A Slip and Fall Is Never “Just an Accident”

When you fall on someone else’s property, the first instinct of the property owner — and their insurance company — is to suggest that the accident was your fault, or simply an unavoidable mishap. In reality, most slip and fall accidents occur because a property owner failed to maintain their premises in a reasonably safe condition, failed to warn visitors of known hazards, or allowed a dangerous condition to persist long enough that they reasonably should have known about it and corrected it. Texas premises liability law places real legal obligations on property owners. When those obligations are breached and you are injured as a result, PM Law Firm holds the responsible party accountable. We serve injured clients throughout Humble, Houston, and Harris County.

Texas Premises Liability Law

The duty a property owner owes to a visitor in Texas depends on the legal status of the visitor at the time of the injury. Texas law recognizes three categories:

Invitee

A person who enters the property with the owner’s express or implied invitation for a purpose connected to the owner’s business — such as a store customer or restaurant patron. Invitees receive the highest duty of care: the owner must inspect the property, discover hazards, and either repair them or warn invitees adequately.

Licensee

A person who enters with permission but for their own purpose rather than the owner’s business interest — such as a social guest. The owner must warn licensees of known hazards the licensee would not reasonably discover themselves, but is not required to inspect for unknown hazards.

Trespasser

A person who enters without permission. Generally, property owners owe trespassers only a duty not to cause willful injury, though special rules apply to child trespassers under the attractive nuisance doctrine.

Why Status Matters

Most slip and fall victims are invitees who receive the highest duty of care. Establishing your visitor status — and the property owner’s specific duty to you — is a critical first step in every premises liability case.

Common Slip and Fall Hazards

Dangerous conditions that cause slip and fall injuries come in many forms. The most common hazards we see in our premises liability cases include:

Wet or Slippery Floors

Spilled liquids, recently mopped floors, leaking refrigeration units, and tracked-in rainwater create slip hazards that property owners must promptly address or warn visitors about.

Uneven Surfaces

Cracked sidewalks, buckled flooring, raised thresholds, uneven transitions between surfaces, and parking lot deterioration are trip hazards that owners must repair or mark clearly.

Poor Lighting

Inadequate lighting in stairwells, parking garages, restrooms, and walkways prevents visitors from seeing and avoiding hazards. Burned-out lights left unreplaced constitute negligent maintenance.

Broken or Missing Handrails

Stairs and ramps require code-compliant handrails. Missing, broken, or improperly installed handrails are a significant safety violation and a common source of serious fall injuries.

Cluttered Walkways

Merchandise, boxes, cables, cords, and equipment left in customer traffic areas create tripping hazards that reasonable property management would prevent.

Defective Flooring

Loose carpet, torn mats, curling rugs, and deteriorating tile that has not been repaired or replaced are property maintenance failures that give rise to slip and fall liability.

Proving Your Slip and Fall Case

Slip and fall cases in Texas require proof that the property owner had actual or constructive knowledge of the hazardous condition. This is the most heavily contested element in premises liability cases. There are three ways to establish this knowledge:

Actual Knowledge

Evidence that the property owner or an employee knew about the specific hazard — for example, an employee who mopped a floor and failed to place a wet floor sign, or a manager who received a complaint about a broken stair and took no action.

Constructive Knowledge

Evidence that the hazard existed long enough that a reasonable property owner, exercising appropriate inspection and maintenance procedures, should have discovered and corrected it before the accident.

Owner-Created Hazard

When the property owner or their employees actively created the hazardous condition — such as stacking merchandise in a walkway or leaving cleaning equipment in a customer aisle — knowledge is presumed.

Evidence We Gather

We obtain surveillance camera footage, maintenance logs, incident reports, employee statements, and prior accident records to establish the owner’s knowledge and the duration of the hazardous condition.

Common Locations for Slip and Fall Accidents

Slip and fall accidents occur in virtually any location, but certain types of premises generate disproportionate numbers of claims:

Grocery & Retail Stores

High foot traffic, frequent spills, and constant stocking activity create numerous slip and trip hazards. Retailers’ own surveillance systems are often the best evidence of how long a hazard existed before the accident.

Restaurants & Bars

Wet floors near drink stations and restrooms, greasy kitchen-adjacent surfaces, and dimly lit dining areas are common sources of slip and fall accidents in food service establishments.

Parking Lots & Garages

Cracked pavement, potholes, unmarked curbs, standing water, and inadequate lighting in parking facilities cause serious fall injuries, particularly at night and in wet weather.

Workplaces

Third-party premises liability claims may be available when a worker is injured on a client’s or contractor’s property due to the property owner’s negligence, independent of workers’ compensation coverage.

What to Do Immediately After a Slip and Fall

The steps you take at the scene of a slip and fall can make or break your legal claim. If you are able to do so after a fall, take the following steps:

Report the Accident

Notify a manager or property owner immediately and ensure an incident report is completed. Request a copy of the report. If they refuse to complete one, document that refusal.

Photograph the Hazard

Before you leave the scene, photograph the specific condition that caused your fall from multiple angles. Also photograph any wet floor signs (or their absence), your surroundings, and your injuries.

Identify Witnesses

Get the name and contact information of any bystanders who saw you fall or who were aware of the hazardous condition before the accident occurred.

Seek Medical Care Immediately

Go to an emergency room or urgent care that day. Slip and fall injuries including hip fractures, back injuries, and head trauma may not produce their full symptoms immediately after the fall.
