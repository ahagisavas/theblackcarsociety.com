Who’s Liable in a Multicar Accident?

When multiple vehicles are involved, liability can be complex. Here’s how fault is determined in Texas.

PM Law Firm

Multicar accidents — sometimes called chain-reaction or pile-up accidents — are among the most complex cases in personal injury law. When three or more vehicles are involved, determining who is liable and to what degree can be extremely complicated. Understanding how Texas handles fault in these situations is essential to protecting your rights.

How Multicar Accidents Typically Happen

The most common type of multicar accident begins with a single negligent act that triggers a chain reaction. For example, a driver who is distracted or speeding rear-ends one car, pushing it into another, which then strikes a third. In these situations, the initial at-fault driver may bear the most liability — but other drivers who contributed to the chain of events may also share responsibility.

Texas Modified Comparative Fault

Texas uses a modified comparative fault system. Each driver involved in the accident can be assigned a percentage of fault based on their contribution to the crash. As long as your percentage of fault is 50% or less, you can recover compensation — but your award is reduced by your share of fault. If you are found more than 50% at fault, you cannot recover anything.

Multiple Insurance Companies

In a multicar accident, you may be dealing with claims against multiple drivers’ insurance policies simultaneously. Each insurance company will conduct its own investigation and attempt to minimize its client’s share of fault. Navigating multiple insurers, conflicting accounts, and competing interests requires an experienced attorney.

Gathering Evidence in Multicar Accidents

Evidence is critical. This includes:

  • Police reports (which often assign preliminary fault)
  • Witness statements
  • Traffic camera footage
  • Dashcam recordings
  • Accident reconstruction experts
  • Physical evidence from the vehicles

The sooner an attorney gets involved, the better the chance of preserving critical evidence before it disappears.
