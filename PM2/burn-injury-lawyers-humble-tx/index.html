Burn Injury Attorneys

Among the Most Painful and Costly Injuries a Person Can Suffer

Severe burn injuries are among the most agonizing, traumatic, and financially devastating experiences a human being can endure. The immediate pain is extreme, but it is only the beginning of an ordeal that may involve weeks or months of hospitalization, multiple surgeries, painful wound debridement, skin grafting, and intensive rehabilitation. Victims often emerge with permanent scarring and disfigurement that affects not only their physical appearance but their psychological well-being, their relationships, and their ability to work. Degrees of Burn Injuries

Medical and legal professionals classify burn injuries by their depth and severity. Understanding the classification of your injury is critical to accurately valuing your claim:

First-Degree Burns

The least severe classification, involving only the outermost layer of skin (epidermis). Characterized by redness, pain, and minor swelling. Typically heal within a week without significant medical intervention, though they remain painful.

Second-Degree Burns

Extend through the epidermis into the dermis layer. Characterized by blistering, intense pain, weeping wounds, and risk of infection. Depending on depth, may require skin grafting and leave permanent scarring, especially over large surface areas.

Third-Degree Burns

Destroy both the epidermis and dermis completely, often reaching into underlying fat tissue. The wound appears white, brown, or leathery. Nerve damage means the burned area itself may be painless, but surrounding areas are excruciating. Almost always require skin grafting and leave permanent, severe scarring.

Fourth-Degree Burns

The most catastrophic classification, extending through all layers of skin into muscle, tendon, and bone. These burns are immediately life-threatening, frequently require amputation, and leave survivors with profound, permanent disability and disfigurement.

Common Causes of Burn Injuries

Burn injuries arise in many different accident contexts. PM Law Firm handles burn injury claims arising from all of the following causes:

Car & Truck Accidents

Post-collision fires caused by ruptured fuel tanks, electrical system failures, or cargo carrying flammable materials are a significant source of severe burn injuries in Texas.

Explosions & Fires

Industrial explosions, gas line failures, propane accidents, and building fires caused by negligent maintenance or defective equipment can cause catastrophic burns to multiple victims simultaneously.

Chemical Burns

Contact with caustic industrial chemicals, cleaning products, or improperly stored hazardous materials can cause chemical burns that are as severe as thermal burns and may penetrate deep tissue.

Workplace Accidents

Workers in the oil and gas, petrochemical, construction, and manufacturing sectors face elevated burn risk. When employer negligence or a third party’s conduct causes the injury, civil claims may supplement workers’ compensation.

Defective Products

Defectively designed appliances, lithium battery fires, faulty electrical wiring in consumer products, and defective automotive components can cause severe burns without any fault on the victim’s part.

Scalding & Scald Burns

Scalding from improperly regulated hot water systems, steam releases, or food and beverage service accidents can cause serious second and third-degree burns, particularly in elderly victims and children.

The Long-Term Impact of Burn Injuries

The consequences of a serious burn injury extend far beyond the initial hospitalization. Victims face a cascade of long-term physical, psychological, and economic challenges that must be fully accounted for in any fair settlement or verdict:

Scarring & Disfigurement

Burn scars — particularly hypertrophic and keloid scars — are permanent, often painful, and can cause contractures that limit joint mobility. Visible facial and body scarring affects every aspect of a victim’s social and professional life.

Multiple Reconstructive Surgeries

Burn survivors often undergo dozens of surgeries over years — skin grafts, scar revisions, contracture releases, and reconstructive procedures — each with its own recovery period, anesthesia risk, and medical cost.

Psychological Trauma

PTSD, depression, anxiety, and body dysmorphia are extraordinarily common among burn survivors. The psychological impact of disfigurement and the trauma of the accident and treatment can be as disabling as the physical injury.

Occupational Impact

Depending on the location and extent of scarring, burn survivors may be unable to return to physically demanding work, face discrimination in client-facing roles, or suffer reduced earning capacity due to physical limitations.
