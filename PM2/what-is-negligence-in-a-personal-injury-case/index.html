What Is Negligence in a Personal Injury Case?

Negligence is the foundation of most personal injury claims. Understanding it is the first step to knowing your rights.

PM Law Firm

In personal injury law, negligence is the legal concept that determines who is responsible for an accident or injury. To win a personal injury case in Texas, you generally must prove that the other party was negligent — and that their negligence caused your injuries and damages.

The Four Elements of Negligence

To establish negligence, your attorney must prove four elements:

  • Duty — the defendant had a legal duty to act with reasonable care toward you.
  • Breach — the defendant breached that duty by acting (or failing to act) in a way that a reasonable person would not.
  • Causation — the defendant’s breach of duty directly caused your injuries.
  • Damages — you suffered actual harm as a result, whether physical, financial, or emotional.

Examples of Negligence in Personal Injury Cases

Negligence takes many forms depending on the type of case:

  • A driver who runs a red light and causes a collision has breached their duty to follow traffic laws.
  • A property owner who fails to clean up a wet floor that causes a customer to slip and fall has breached their duty to maintain safe premises.
  • A trucking company that allows a driver to exceed legal hours-of-service limits has breached federal safety regulations.

Comparative Negligence in Texas

Texas follows a modified comparative negligence rule. This means that if you were partially at fault for the accident, your compensation is reduced by your percentage of fault. However, if you are found to be more than 50% at fault, you cannot recover any compensation at all. Insurance companies frequently try to assign partial fault to accident victims to reduce what they owe — an experienced attorney can counter these tactics.

Proving Negligence

Building a negligence case requires evidence. This may include:

  • Police reports
  • Witness statements
  • Photographs of the scene and injuries
  • Medical records
  • Expert testimony
  • Surveillance footage
  • Physical evidence from the accident

Why Negligence Matters to Your Case

Understanding negligence matters because it determines whether you have a valid claim and how much compensation you can recover. If multiple parties share fault — as is common in multi-vehicle accidents — an experienced attorney can help identify all liable parties and maximize your recovery.
