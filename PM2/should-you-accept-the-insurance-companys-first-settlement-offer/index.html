Should You Accept the Insurance Company’s First Settlement Offer?

The first offer is almost never the best offer. Here’s why — and what to do instead.

PM Law Firm

After a car accident or personal injury, it can feel like a relief when the insurance company calls quickly and offers you a settlement. The process seems simple — they offer you money, you sign some paperwork, and the ordeal is over. But accepting the first settlement offer from an insurance company is almost always a mistake, and here’s why.

Why Insurance Companies Offer Quick Settlements

Insurance companies are businesses, and their goal is to pay out as little as possible on every claim. When an adjuster calls you quickly with a settlement offer, it is usually because they know — or suspect — that your claim is worth more than they are offering. They want to resolve the claim before you fully understand the extent of your injuries, before you have consulted an attorney, and before you have time to research what your claim is actually worth.

The Problem with Signing Early

When you accept a settlement and sign a release, you are typically waiving your right to pursue any additional compensation — forever. This means that if your injuries turn out to be more serious than initially apparent, if you develop complications down the road, or if you need additional surgeries or treatment, you will have no recourse. The release you signed closes the case permanently.

Medical Treatment Takes Time to Understand

One of the biggest mistakes accident victims make is settling before they have reached maximum medical improvement (MMI). Until your treating physicians have a clear picture of your long-term prognosis, it is impossible to accurately calculate the full value of your claim — including future medical costs, long-term disability, and ongoing pain and suffering.

How to Evaluate a Settlement Offer

Before accepting any offer, you should understand:

  • The full extent of your injuries and your long-term prognosis
  • All past and future medical expenses
  • Lost wages — past and future
  • Pain and suffering and emotional distress
  • Property damage and other out-of-pocket costs

An experienced personal injury attorney can help you evaluate whether an offer is fair or whether pushing back — or filing suit — will result in significantly better compensation.
