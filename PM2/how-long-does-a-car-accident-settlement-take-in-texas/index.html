How Long Does a Car Accident Settlement Take in Texas?

Timelines vary widely depending on the complexity of your case. Here’s what to expect.

PM Law Firm

One of the most common questions we hear from car accident clients is: how long is this going to take? The honest answer is that it depends on a number of factors, including the severity of your injuries, whether liability is disputed, and whether your case settles out of court or goes to trial.

The Typical Settlement Timeline

Simple cases with clear liability and minor injuries can sometimes settle in a few months. More complex cases — especially those involving serious injuries, disputed liability, or multiple parties — can take one to three years or longer. Here is a general breakdown of the stages:

  • Medical treatment and reaching maximum medical improvement (MMI)
  • Attorney investigation and demand letter
  • Insurance company review and negotiation
  • Settlement or filing suit
  • Discovery and litigation
  • Trial (if necessary)

Why Reaching Maximum Medical Improvement Matters

One of the most important milestones in a personal injury case is reaching maximum medical improvement (MMI) — the point at which your doctor determines that your condition has stabilized and further significant improvement is unlikely. Settling before MMI means you may not know the full extent of your injuries or future medical costs. A settlement reached too early can leave you undercompensated.

Factors That Can Slow Down a Settlement

  • Disputed liability — when the other driver or their insurer argues you were at fault, resolving the dispute takes time.
  • Serious injuries — high-value claims receive more scrutiny from insurance companies.
  • Multiple parties — when more than one driver or entity is involved, negotiations become more complex.
  • Litigation — if a fair settlement cannot be reached, filing suit significantly extends the timeline but often results in better outcomes.

The Statute of Limitations in Texas

Texas law generally gives you two years from the date of the accident to file a personal injury lawsuit. If you miss this deadline, you lose your right to sue entirely. This makes it critical to consult an attorney promptly — even if you believe your case will settle out of court.
